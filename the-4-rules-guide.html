The 4 Rules of Core Control

The 4 Rules That Make Your Core Strong, Not Stiff

Most people who train brace harder and still feel weak. These are the 4 Rules to follow if you want your core to finally do its job, so your strength feels natural instead of forced.

Why Does Your Core Still Feel Weak?

Yet another core workout has failed. Of course, you blame yourself or, perhaps, your body. You did the ab work, took the Pilates classes, lifted heavier, maybe even paid a trainer, and your core still feels weak, stiff or switched off.

I’ve heard this story hundreds of times and you’ll be relieved to hear that most of the time it’s not you or your body. It’s your control.

What do I mean when I say it’s your control?

I’m talking about how your body coordinates tension, breath and movement. Your core should be able to let go, breathe, adjust its tension, and move in parts. The problem is, very few people who train can pull it off.

After you read this short guide, though, you won’t be one of those people. Embodying the 4 Rules of Core Control will put you in the rare group of people who lift, run and move in a way that looks effortless, because it is.

I’m obsessed with helping people who train find strength that feels natural. I learned it the hard way: years of bracing everything, until two herniated discs in my neck made me relearn how strength works. Now I teach it, and I’m about to help you.

Along the way I’ve figured out the 4 Rules that make a core work. Bodies that break these rules grip, compensate and stall.

These rules work whether you lift heavy, do Pilates, run, or are coming back from a setback. Embody the 4 Rules and you’ll be able to see why an exercise works for you or against you.

Three things happen when you embody these rules:

You feel it.

You start feeling your deep core work, not just your neck and lower back.

Your lifts change.

Weight that used to feel forced starts to feel smooth and controlled.

Your body lasts.

You stop fighting your body and start building strength you can keep for decades.

Fair warning: once you know the 4 Rules, every way you’ve been forcing your training will become obvious. The good news is you will know how to fix it, and a body that stops breaking these rules has a serious advantage. Let’s start with the rule people break the most.

01
Rule 01

Learn to Let Go

You can’t control tension if you don’t know how to release it.

Nobody can build strength on top of constant tension. If your belly is held in all day, your glutes grip and your hip flexors pull your pelvis forward, your deep core has nowhere to go. The moment you add more effort on top, you’ve lost it.

Around my studio I have a mantra: “Let go before you go.”

“Squeeze your core” sounds strong. “Let your belly soften, then build support” actually works. One adds tension on top of tension. The other gives your core room to switch on.

Think of a fist you never open. You can’t make it grip any harder. You can only make it tired. Your core works the same way. When you learn to release first, every contraction after that has somewhere to go, and your training finally starts working for you.

DEFINITION

RESTING TENSION is how hard your muscles work when you’re not asking them to. The higher your resting tension, the less control you have when you need it. The lower your resting tension, the more strength you can actually use. Every time.

Learn to let go and watch your core switch on.

Examples

Situation✕Always Holding✓Able to Let Go
Before a deadlift✕“Jaw, neck and abs braced before you even touch the bar.”✓“Settled first. Tension builds as you pull the slack out of the bar.”
Pilates class✕“Pelvic floor squeezed for the whole hour.”✓“Pelvic floor lifts and lets go with your breath.”
Between sets✕“Shoulders still up by your ears, breath fast and high.”✓“Shoulders drop, breath slows, body resets.”
Sitting at your desk✕“Hip flexors tight, belly held, breathing into the chest.”✓“Sitting supported, belly free to move.”
EXERCISE

Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet flat. Take 5 slow breaths. Notice your belly, your glutes, your pelvic floor and your jaw. Notice every place that stays held on the inhale. Those are the places your core can’t use yet. Then breathe out slowly and let each one go, one at a time.

02
Rule 02

Make Room to Breathe

Let your breath expand in every direction.

Your breath is the engine of your core. Your diaphragm sits at the top, your pelvic floor at the bottom, and your deep abs wrap around the sides. Every inhale expands that whole system. When it can expand in every direction (front, sides and back), your core gets pressure it can use. When it can’t, the pressure goes somewhere else: up into your neck and shoulders, or down into your pelvic floor. Make room for your breath, and your core gets stronger without adding a single exercise.

A lifter can take a huge gulp of air into the chest and shoulders before every rep. Or she can let her ribs widen to the sides and back, low belly first. The first one looks strong and feels strained. The second one builds real support. You were never short on breath. You were short on room.

DEFINITION

A 360° BREATH is when your ribs and belly expand in every direction (front, sides and back) without your neck or shoulders doing the work. The more directions your breath can reach, the more support your core has. The fewer directions, the more you grip.

Make room for your breath and watch your strength grow.

Examples

Situation✕Blocked Breath✓360° Breath
Before a squat✕“A big gulp of air into the chest. Shoulders rise.”✓“Ribs widen to the sides and back. Shoulders stay quiet.”
Plank✕“Breath held until the timer runs out.”✓“Slow breaths. Belly, sides and back keep moving.”
Overhead press✕“Ribs flare forward, lower back arches.”✓“Ribs stay down, breath fills down and back mainly.”
Pilates Hundred✕“Belly pushed out, neck straining.”✓“Breath into the sides, belly quiet and supported.”
EXERCISE

Sit tall or lie on your back. Put your hands on your lower back ribs and breathe in. Do your hands move? Now try your side ribs. Then your low belly. Write down which areas move and which stay still. Every area that stays still is a direction your breath can’t reach yet, and that’s where your support is missing.

03
Rule 03

Control the Tension

Think dial, not ON/OFF switch.

Most people who train have two settings: off, or everything. They brace the same way for a light plank as for a heavy deadlift, and their neck, jaw and lower back pay the price. Can you learn to do it differently? Definitely.

Strong movers turn their tension up and down to match the task, and they keep breathing while they do it. The goal was never more tension. The goal is the right amount, in the right place, at the right moment.

Around my studio we say: “Dial, not switch.” A switch only has two positions, and both are wrong most of the time. A dial lets you choose. Light support to walk. Medium support for a dead bug. High support for a heavy lift. And always enough room left to breathe. When your tension matches the task, you stop wasting energy and your neck and lower back stop paying the bill.

A lifter can squeeze everything at 100% on every rep, from warm-up to top set. Or she can build tension step by step and save her full brace for the heaviest moment. The first one gets tired, tight and stuck. The second one gets stronger, because her effort goes exactly where the weight needs it.

DEFINITION

THE TENSION DIAL is your ability to choose how much core tension a movement needs (20%, 50%, 80%) and hold it while you breathe. The better your dial, the less energy you waste and the stronger you get. The worse your dial, the more you grip.

Control your tension and watch your lifts feel lighter.

Examples

Movement✕ON/OFF Switch✓Dial
Dead bug✕“Full brace, breath held, neck tight.”✓“Steady medium support, breathing through every rep.”
Kettlebell swing✕“Locked at 100% from start to finish.”✓“Snaps up at the top, softens at the bottom.”
Heavy deadlift✕“Neck and jaw braced harder than the core.”✓“Core high, neck and jaw quiet.”
Push-up✕“Hips sag, or everything shakes.”✓“Just enough tension for a straight line. No more.”
EXERCISE

Lie on your back with your knees bent. Brace your core gently, about 20%, and hold it for 3 breaths. Now go to 50%. Then 80%. Can you feel three different levels? Can you breathe at each one? If you can only find “off” and “everything,” your dial is your starting point. Practise the middle levels until you can feel them without thinking.

04
Rule 04

Move Independently

Move your ribs, pelvis and limbs without always moving them together.

Your ribs and your pelvis are designed to move on their own. When they only move together, like one stiff block, something else has to cover for them. Usually that’s your lower back, your hip flexors or your neck. That’s why so many strong people still feel their lifts in the wrong places. Teach your ribs and pelvis to move independently, and force finally travels where it should.

This rule is less about strength and more about coordination. Stiff bodies move in blocks, mostly by bracing everything together. Controlled bodies move in parts, letting each piece do its own job.

Here’s one way to tell whether your ribs and pelvis move independently. Lie on your back and tilt your pelvis forward and back. Watch your ribs. If they lift and flare every time your pelvis moves, they’re moving as one block, and your lower back is doing the work for both.

You can still brace hard when a lift asks for it, but when you do, make sure it comes after you can move each part on its own. Control first. Then stiffness.

A Pilates student can roll down by dropping from the middle of her back. Or she can peel down one segment at a time. The second one works because every part of her spine takes its share of the load.

DEFINITION

INDEPENDENT CONTROL is your ability to move your ribs without your pelvis following, and your pelvis without your ribs following. Strong trunks aren’t stiff blocks. They’re chains you control link by link. Never move as a block. Always move in parts.

Move your ribs and pelvis independently, and your lifts stop leaking.

Examples

Movement✕Moves as a Block✓Moves Independently
Roll-down✕“Drops in the middle, feet lift off the floor.”✓“Peels down one segment at a time.”
Overhead reach✕“Lower back arches to get the arms up.”✓“Arms rise, ribs and pelvis stay stacked.”
Hip hinge✕“Lower back rounds along with the hips.”✓“Hips travel back, spine stays long.”
Deep squat✕“Tailbone tucks hard at the bottom, ribs flare on the way up.”✓“Pelvis follows the thighs, ribs stay over the pelvis.”
EXERCISE

Lie on your back with your hands on your lower ribs. Slowly tilt your pelvis forward and back. Do your ribs stay still? Then sit tall and roll down slowly to the floor, breathing out. Mark the point where you drop or your feet lift. That point is where your control ends today, and where your training starts.
