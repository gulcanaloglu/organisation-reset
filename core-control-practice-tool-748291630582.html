Part 1

Find Your Starting Point

You’ll do 5 short checks lying on your back: the Self-Check first, then one check for each of the 4 Rules. For each check, follow the steps, do it slowly for a few breaths, then answer the question underneath by tapping what was closest to what you felt. There are no wrong answers. Your answers decide what goes into your practice.

0

Self-Check

Your baseline. You repeat it at the end.

The check: one-leg heel slide · about 30 seconds
  1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat on the floor, hip-width apart.
  2. Slide one hand, palm down, into the gap between your lower back and the floor, just above your belt line. Notice how much your back presses down on your hand.
  3. Breathe out and slowly slide one heel along the floor, away from you, so that leg starts to straighten.
  4. Stop the moment any of these happens: the pressure on your hand gets lighter or heavier, your pelvis rocks or twists, your ribs lift, or you hold your breath.
  5. Look at how far your heel got, then slide it back to the start.

Check Again

Do the heel slide again, right after your practice, and compare.

The re-check: anchored heel slides
  1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Slide one hand, palm down, into the gap between your lower back and the floor.
  2. Tilt your pelvis toward 12 and toward 6 a few times, then let it settle in the middle, where it feels natural. That’s your anchor.
  3. Keep that pelvis position and the same pressure on your hand, and slowly slide one heel away and back. Do 4 to 6 slides on each side.
  4. Notice how far your heel goes before the pressure changes, your pelvis rocks, your ribs lift or you hold your breath.
